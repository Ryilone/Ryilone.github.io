MacroMaxx

A macro tracker for iPhone that works without internet.

  • iPhone
  • SwiftUI, SQLite, HealthKit, WidgetKit
  • Built with Claude and Codex
  • Build 17

Today and Trends, running in the iOS simulator with sample data.

Why I made it

Most calorie and macro apps put the useful parts, like barcode scanning, behind a subscription. Tracking what you eat is basic health stuff. I didn't want to pay every month for it, and I don't think anyone should have to.

It isn't on the App Store, because Apple's developer program costs $99 a year. For now it runs on my phone through a free account.

What it does

Food search runs against USDA's Branded Foods database, stored on the phone in SQLite, so it works in airplane mode. Once a week it checks whether USDA has published a newer release.

Scanning a barcode looks the product up on Open Food Facts. If Open Food Facts doesn't have a Nutri-Score grade for it, the app works one out from the label and combines it with how processed the food is and which additives it has, for a score out of 100.

Restaurant menus come from each chain's own nutrition page or PDF. That's 1,054 items from seven chains, and each one keeps a note of where it came from and when it was read.

It also pulls weigh-ins from Apple Health, has a home screen widget, and has a "What fits" list of foods that fit what you have left for the day.

Unknown isn't zero

This is the decision I'd most want to talk about. If a label doesn't list fiber, that food doesn't have zero fiber. Nobody knows how much it has. A lot of trackers quietly count missing numbers as zero, which makes your totals look better than they are.

MacroMaxx keeps "unknown" and "zero" apart all the way from the database to the screen. When a day's total includes a food with missing data, the app says so instead of showing a clean number.

    Checking the Nutri-Score math

    The Nutri-Score code follows Open Food Facts' own 2023 rules, including the special cases for drinks, cheese and fats. To check it, I compared its grades with the ones Open Food Facts publishes. They matched on 184 of 185 products. The one mismatch was a nut mix whose category wasn't tagged as nuts. 48 of those products are now a permanent test.

    Keeping it on my phone for free

    Apps installed with a free Apple account stop opening after seven days. A script on my Mac runs every day and re-signs the app when it has less than two and a half days left. Before every install it backs up the app's data, and afterward it checks the data is byte-for-byte the same, so a new build can't wipe my history.

    What's not done

    Barcode scanning with the camera has only been tested in the simulator so far. And there's no App Store release until I pay for the developer account or find a free way to share it.
