ClassPath

Your next class, and when to leave for it.

Today and Schedule from the web preview. The classes are made up.

Why I made it

I started ClassPath right before my first semester at LMU. I wanted one screen that tells me what's next and when I need to leave, without making an account or paying for anything.

What works now

You can add classes with more than one meeting time and set term dates. Everything is saved on the phone in SQLite. Today shows the class you're in or the next one, and Schedule shows your week. The project has 375 automated tests.

The routing engine is written and tested, but it isn't hooked up to the screens yet. That's next.

How the routing works

This is the part I'm learning right now, so this is how I'd explain it.

The campus becomes a graph. Every building door and walkway corner is a point, and every path between two points has a cost, which is how long it takes to walk. To find a route, the app uses A* search. It spreads out from where you are, but it always tries the paths that look closest to your class first, using the straight-line distance as its guess. That finds the fastest route without checking every path on campus.

Once it knows the walking time, the leave-by time is just your class start minus the walk.

Try it. This is a made-up campus, not LMU.

Outlined points are on A*'s list to check, filled ones are checked. Tap a building to walk there, or tap a path to close it like it's under construction.

Why it won't guess

Campus maps come in data packs. A pack only turns on if its SHA-256 fingerprint is on an approved list, so a broken or edited file can't send you the wrong way. There's no approved LMU pack yet, so the app says walking time isn't available. I'd rather it say that than make something up.

Color rules

Before building any screens I wrote down what each color means. Blue is something you can tap, green is verified, amber means you might be late, and red means delete. Nothing else gets a color. A class's color is a thin stripe on the edge of its card, and nothing is shown with color alone, so it still works for people who are colorblind.
